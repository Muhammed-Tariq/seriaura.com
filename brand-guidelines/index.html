01 · Approved forms

This is what it looks like.

Use the supplied artwork, with its lettering, hearts and proportions intact. The white version is the everyday signature. Orange is for when the signature has had a good day.

02 · Personal space

The hearts need room.

Leave at least one heart-height of clear space around the visible artwork. Measure from the logo, not the transparent edges of the image file. No text, borders or other logos inside this zone.

04 · Background checks

05 · Chromatic jurisdiction

Orange is an accent, not a land grab.

The page colours have specific jobs. Charcoal holds the space, paper carries the words, grey handles the small print, and orange gets your attention. It needn't keep it indefinitely.

These are the site colours. The orange logo keeps its original gradient; a colour swatch is not permission to repaint it.

06 · Interpersonal alignment

Working with another person.

Keep each identity intact, leave space between them, and aim for equal visual weight. Align what you can see, rather than the invisible edges of the image files.

“Another Person” is a fictional stand-in. No actual person has agreed to this arrangement.

07 · Applied overthinking

It has to live somewhere.

A good application gives the mark a clear place and lets the rest of the design do its job. Not every surface needs another logo. Some surfaces can just be surfaces.
