For progeny and posterity.

Characterising myself has never come easily. I believe that everybody I know has some mental model of myself that approximates, but never matches, who I really am, and I'm no exception to that. A famous William James quote goes, "a man has as many social selves as there are individuals who recognise him and carry an image of him in their mind," and the feeling that this overwhelmingly evokes in me is that of being misunderstood. I hope that this website will make people understand who I am better.

I usually go by the moniker Seriaura on the internet, but I've also been known as Seriorance, Deceitful, Vyclic, iiSeveranceS, Xxawesomeguy2, and awesomepower21467000, courtesy of some Roblox YouTube channels I made when I was 11. For the time being, I'm indifferent to whether you use my moniker or my real name.

Recently, I've hiked Seven Sisters, flown to Prague twice for the Arb Retreat and the FABRIC alumni reunion (a wonderful joy), attended EAGxOxford (which entailed racing the Vitality 10K in London and immediately commuting to Oxford thereafter), and had a lot more impromptu fun. Currently, I'm studying for the TMUA (take two) and returning to running; soon, I'll be watching a special flavour of improvised comedy at the Hammersmith Apollo, reuniting one last time with my Impact First group, and watching the finale of Jet Lag's Japanorama.

If you'd like to see an overview of my career, education, or projects, my uppercase website's likely what you're looking for, albeit with some overlap.

Musings

I really like:
  • listening to liquid or dancefloor drum-and-bass — in particular, Liquicity's flavour of it. I struggle to describe why I like this in particular, but it seems to evoke such a broad spectrum of feelings: melancholy, euphoria, emotion, overwhelm, nostalgia, and so much more; it all took me to solo-tripping Liquicity Festival this year (just three months after I'd turned 18) and it was incredible;
  • long-distance running — I've ran five half-marathons (for now, just local ones: twice Hampton Court, Surrey, Ealing, and the Martian Race), and I'm also running the Bath Half and the Brighton Marathon soon;
  • travelling — I have become obsessed with the ineffable feeling of liberation that comes with getting lost in a different country, and I'd be super keen to discuss how this manifests in you;
  • self-expression — I blog irregularly, I've created two personal websites (including this), I've made actual Seriaura merch, and I'm really interested in how I can be more "me";
  • graphic design — I have a painstaking attention to detail; I've spent hours on random projects I ended up loving and, in gleeful pride, staring at for comparable lengths of time — it's a pity I'm slowly growing obsolete;
  • miscellaneously — pretending that I have a personal brand, adoring the Josh-isms, counting down to things, creating text art, and Jet Lag: The Game;
  • talking to people. DM me!
I'm currently on a gap year — something I'm using as an excuse to say yes to everything. I chose to do so because, over the span of my entire life, being able to guarantee a year of freedom and enjoyment seems like far too promising a deal to pass on. I've also done so because I've experienced an incessant volley of setbacks over the past year, and I see this year as an opportunity to do all the things I previously wasn't able to, and then some. Some of my more ambitious goals include completing Ironman Wales, hosting a game of Jet Lag across the UK, running the Brighton Marathon in under 3 hours, and doing some technical AI safety research. I value most the prospect of having loads of fun, though, and the vast majority of the activities I'm undertaking pursue this as an end. I strongly believe that this has a sort of positive feedback loop that does wonders for me in so many other facets of life, not least because I'm young and neuroplastic.

Right now, my favourite song is Language by Porter Robinson. I formerly looped Koncept's remix (which is at such a faster pace than the original now that I've become really accustomed to it), and it took me a while to discover how emotional this song really was. It evokes so strongly the feeling of preparing to start something hard, something meaningful; the feeling of having spent months or years on an work of ineffable importance and its emotionally charged release. There's a strong element of nostalgia, too; some say this is too reminiscent of 2016 Roblox tycoon music for their liking, but for those who don't, it's some of the most euphoric, melancholic music one could ever hear. I (non-exhaustively) also listen to Japanese death metal, depressive breakcore, atmospheric drum-and-bass, J-pop, vocaloid, alternative rock, heavy metal, and white girl music.

Thousings

Happiness feels more like my baseline than it ever has before. As a result, it's currently really hard for me to picture feeling viscerally uncertain or scared about something — for example, I scheduled an email to be sent to myself at the end of 2026 with all the (insanely ambitous) goals I had from the start of 2025, and I've achieved probably none of them. In fact, my former self would probably not feel very happy if I laid out the facts to him, stripping away any information about how I felt about it now. I don't really know what's happened since, but despite not having succeeded there, I'm the happiest I've ever been. This is all to say that it's hard for me to feel paralysing uncertainty; it could be because I'm a lot more secure in myself, that I've developed an unruly tolerance for failure, or both. If you held me at gunpoint and asked me what I was uncertain on, however, I'd probably say some of the stuff below.

Will I have to constantly varietymaxx to sustain happiness well into my thirties or fourties? Does studying while on a semaglutide-induced calorie deficit negatively impact focus or knowledge retention? How do I actually organise a rave at my 19th birthday party, (possibly) with a VJ and all? As opposed to other qualities, how much does an overlap of interests actually matter when it comes to finding a partner? When do I decide that something is important enough to me to make it a part of my identity? Can this website be improved in any way (particularly in design)? Is there some other genre of music I'm just missing out on because I'm so narrowly into drum-and-bass? Is it possible to strike a balance between working really hard and being in a consistently great mood, or does one have to come at the expense of the other? A lot of people feel as though they'll have to work a job that they don't care about, for someone they don't care about, for something that doesn't do any good in the world — why haven't more people tried to find something different for themselves?

If you've read this far, do feel free to email me; like many others, I enjoy hearing from people who, in an act of Tier 3 stalking or not, happen to come across my blog, irrespective of what they have to say (barring anything weird). If that stops being true, I will delete the past two sentences, including this one.
