### Substack blog

The blog uses the site's colours, Sentient typography, sidebar, footer and interactive footnotes. Article body text uses the same Sentient typeface with rem-based reading sizes and a 1.5 line height so browser zoom enlarges the text instead of being cancelled by viewport scaling. The importer moves leading and trailing link whitespace outside the anchor, including whitespace nested inside spans. Article pages have no ribbon. The desktop reading column occupies the exact middle third of the viewport; tablets and phones keep a readable full-width column. Letter spacing is normal, with medium-weight titles. Article images remain in their original positions inside posts.

The temporary ribbon and post-layout selectors and their scripts have been removed. Old `ribbon`/`view` query parameters and saved preview choices have no effect. Article pages do not load the ribbon renderer or request ribbon text; Home retains its ribbon beside the writing index.

Run `npm ci`, then `npm run sync:blog` to refresh the public Substack feed. To rebuild from a saved RSS file, run `node tools/sync-substack.cjs --feed-file path/to/feed.xml`. The importer sanitizes remote HTML, converts footnotes to the site's format, keeps images and captions, replaces embedded players with links, removes subscription forms, and routes links between imported articles locally. Reading and article navigation work without JavaScript. Posts use their original Substack canonical URLs to identify the source; comments and subscriptions remain on Substack. Images are served from their original hosts.

`writings/posts.json` retains older posts after they leave Substack's rolling RSS feed. Posts still present in the feed are updated on each sync. Deleted or unpublished posts must be removed from this cache and their generated directory deliberately; the importer does not infer deletion from a missing feed entry. Only content made available in the public feed is imported. For an older archive beyond that feed, import a saved RSS export first. Edit source posts on Substack, and edit article styles in `blog.css`, the Home index in `writings.css`, and the importer's template; generated HTML is replaced on refresh, including the marked Writings list inside `index.html`.

`.github/workflows/pages.yml` refreshes the blog and publishes the static site on pushes to `main`, daily at 06:23 UTC, or with **Run workflow**. It commits the refreshed archive so posts are retained over time, then deploys only public site files. To activate this after pushing, choose **GitHub Actions** under the repository's **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source** and retain the existing custom domain. Branch rules must allow the workflow's archive commits. A failed fetch/invalid feed stops publication and keeps the current live site. No deployment occurs merely by editing these files locally.

Preview with `node tools/serve.cjs` and open `http://127.0.0.1:4173/index.html#writings`. `npm run test:blog` starts its own temporary server and checks real posts, sanitization, local navigation, footnotes, the photo-and-writings layout at six widths, legacy redirects, section anchors, article-only ribbon exclusions, and no-JavaScript reading in installed Microsoft Edge. It saves screenshots in `.preview-refresh/`.

Use ordinary HTML: `<em>...</em>` or `<i>...</i>` for italics, `<strong>...</strong>` or `<b>...</b>` for bold, and `<a href="https://example.com">link text</a>` for links. Save `index.html` and refresh to preview. Photo positions, countdown data, and ribbon settings remain in `content.js`.

`assets/text-art.txt` contains the small words on the ribbon itself, separately from all body copy.

## Footnotes

Put a template inside a footnote marker after the words you want to annotate:

```html
<p>A sentence worth explaining<sup data-footnote><template>
  I'm using "ordinary quotes", <em>italics</em>, and <strong>bold</strong>.
  Here's a <a href="https://example.com">working link</a>.
</template></sup>.</p>
```

The note is ordinary HTML inside `<template>...</template>`, so apostrophes and double quotes in its text need no escaping. Formatting, links, paragraphs, and lists work as they do elsewhere on the page. Use `&lt;` to display a literal less-than sign and `&amp;` for a literal ampersand. The template stays hidden until its contents are shown in the popup.

On Home and information pages, `footnotes.js` numbers notes automatically in reading order. Hover over a number and move onto the box to read or click its links. Keyboard focus opens the note; Tab enters its links, Shift+Tab returns to the reference, and Escape closes it and returns focus from the popup to its number. On touchscreens, tap the number to open it and tap again or elsewhere to close it. External links open in a new tab, following the site's usual link behaviour. There is no footnotes section at the bottom. Existing footnote URLs still lead to the number in the text.

Articles additionally load `article-notes.js`: notes remain visible in the right margin above 1100px, with small orange numbers, muted text, and a fine left rule. Notes align with their references and move down to avoid one another; the article reserves room for the final note. Wider columns and a 1.55 line height improve longer notes, whose lengthy paragraphs are split at sentence boundaries without changing the text or inline links. All notes use upright text. Bars remain grey, fade to orange while a note or its reference is hovered, and fade back on exit; desktop references are not permanently styled as expanded disclosures. On smaller screens or a zoomed visual viewport, references use the same hover/focus/tap popups as Home; Escape and outside taps dismiss them. Backlinks return focus to the reference. Fonts, images, and viewport changes trigger a new layout. Run `node tools/verify-article-notes.cjs` to check note spacing, responsive disclosures, botanical symbols, pin placement, sidebar centring, and link colours. `node tools/verify-scroll-rendering.cjs` checks cached ribbon pixels, rapid scrolling, prepared photos, fixed navigation, and the gradient logo.

Use this markup in any collection or standalone page. Notes can be refreshed with the existing `collectionchange` event. Older plain-text `data-footnote="..."` attributes remain supported, but use the template form for new or formatted notes. All existing pages load `footnotes.css` and `footnotes.js`; include both when creating another page.

The “Josh-isms” link now opens `josh-isms/index.html`. Add its writing below the heading, where the HTML comment marks the space for content.

## Moving the Polaroids

All 19 displayed photographs are configured in the `polaroids` list in `content.js`. Each entry has a readable `id` and the original filename in its `src`, so it is easy to find a particular picture. The video frame has been removed from the collage.

The 17-photo main collage is scattered in staggered groups with varied tilts, light overlaps, and clear space beside the ribbon. The mountain landscape, sunset cliffs, festival entrance, and group photographs have larger frames; the SIRI LIFE chart, Dune game, museum mirror selfie, and Brixton board have extra emphasis. The computer-screen photograph has been removed. On phones and tablets, a compact overlapping composition keeps all frames at one shared scale, with no full-width enlarged rows. The narrow-screen positions are --compact-x/y in mediaqueries.css; the closing portraits retain their separate layout.

The main collage uses `gallery: 'opening'`. The childhood suit and orange-suit portraits use `gallery: 'closing'` and the supplied full-resolution JPEGs. They sit below the final paragraph, tilted −7° and +6°. The orange frame is 330 × 543 design pixels, cropped closely around the hat and crossed arms. The childhood portrait keeps its original crop and scales its 300 × 390 frame and borders to 90%, slightly overlapping in front of the orange portrait. The whole pair is displayed at 85% scale and centred between the ribbon's right edge at the portraits' midpoint and the full page's right edge, using the tilted frames' visible bounds to balance the gaps. On phones they stack with a small overlap. Their space grows with the writing so they clear the paragraph and footer.

| Field | What to edit |
| --- | --- |
| `gallery` | `opening` for the main collage, or `closing` for the final pair of portraits |
| `x`, `y` | Legacy percentage positions; the desktop collage now uses `--collage-x` and `--collage-y` in `style.css`, while smaller screens use --compact-x/y in mediaqueries.css. |
| `width`, `height` | Outer frame size in design pixels; scaled automatically for the screen |
| `rotation` | Degrees; negative tilts left, positive tilts right |
| `crop` | Image focal point, e.g. `'50% 35%'` keeps more of the upper part visible |
| `cropBox` | `[left, top, width, height]` of the selected rectangle, as percentages of the original image; leave out for ordinary `crop` positioning |
| `layer` | Higher values place a frame above overlapping neighbours; group photos and SIRI LIFE use `2` |
| `quarterTurn` | Image-only rotation in quarter turns; `-1` turns the Liquicity tent photo upright counterclockwise. Use without `cropBox`. |
| `alt` | A short description for screen readers |

For example, to move the sunset photo right and down, find `[data-photo="sunset"]` in `style.css` and increase its `--collage-x` and `--collage-y`. To show more sky, add `crop: '50% 25%'` to its entry in `content.js`.

To move the whole desktop collage, edit `.scrapbook` in `style.css`. It starts at `left: 20%` with `width: 54%`; its top follows the end of the preceding writing with a 40-design-pixel gap. Rotated frame edges stay inside the preceding text’s margins, with the lower rows tucked upward to preserve ribbon clearance. `.polaroids` defines the 1430-unit canvas height and scales its coordinates at 68% of the page's design scale. Smaller-screen scaling is in `mediaqueries.css`.

The group photos use larger frames with crops that retain everyone. SIRI LIFE uses a 260 × 211 frame focused on the sheet. Dune is cropped to the tabletop to exclude feet. These are display crops; the source photographs are untouched. When changing frame sizes with `cropBox`, keep the inner image area's proportions close to the selected crop. The frame has 12px top/side borders and a 43px bottom border in design units.

## Layout and motion


There are two compositions: above 1100px the bowed ribbon, typography, sidebar spacing, photos and footer scale together from a 1920px-wide design; at 1100px and below the separate mobile/tablet layout keeps a horizontal ribbon above the reading column. The permanent design is the leaning wave: an asymmetric sweep that spends more time on the left. Its analytic slope drives the lettering and text clearance, and its shape is independent of text length and viewport rounding. The comparison designs and picker have been removed; earlier saved preview selections have no effect. The interests list sits below the first bend to leave room for the curve. Lists use outside markers and hanging indentation so wrapped lines align with their text.

Only the ribbon depth needed for the page is generated, rather than an 18,000px path. The entrance animation releases its whole-page opacity layer once complete. Polaroid images remain mounted and eagerly loaded; changing scroll position does not remove them. Footer icons use ordinary SVG images instead of transformed CSS masks, and the navigation flower is a vector asset, so controls do not depend on emoji rendering.

The ribbon gradually widens towards its middle, then narrows towards the end. Every track uses the same off-white colour throughout, with no darkening gradient. The default `ribbonStyle: 'warped'` in `content.js` keeps the characters distorted around the bends but controls their height separately from row spacing. Letter height varies from 90% to 114% of the original font height; rows are 9.75–16.5 design pixels apart on desktop (about 69% widening). This keeps the ribbon compact without tall letters or large gaps. The existing text clearance is preserved. Each page's visible length determines the taper, while desktop dimensions still scale together. On phones and tablets the taper runs from left to right along the horizontal ribbon.

To restore the previous appearance, change `ribbonStyle: 'warped'` to `ribbonStyle: 'spacing'` in `content.js` and refresh. That renderer is retained: it varies the spacing between tracks while keeping each character's original shape. Switch back to `'warped'` at any time; both styles use the same curve and colour.

The warped lettering drifts slowly along the fixed curve, with all rows moving downwards on desktop (leftwards on mobile), at slightly different speeds. Set `ribbonMovement: 'alternating'` in `content.js` and refresh to restore the previous opposite-direction movement; the default is `'together'`. `ribbon-renderer.js` draws an immutable curved mesh using a cached font atlas and a repeating character lookup texture. Animation only updates the shader clock; it does not change thousands of SVG text nodes or upload new geometry each frame. It follows the screen's refresh rate. An animated canvas uses a buffered, quantized drawing region at up to 2× pixel density, avoiding allocation on every scroll position. Narrow cached tiles cover the whole river beneath it so fast compositor scrolling never exposes an unprepared section; these are regenerated only when the mesh changes. The animated surface masks the cached layer to prevent doubled lettering. Polaroid images load eagerly and decode before the page is ready. Desktop rows extend 240 design pixels above the page and are clipped by the page boundary, so they run off the top edge. Motion pauses in a hidden tab and stops when reduced motion is requested; resizing preserves its elapsed phase. Offscreen artwork skips drawing. Browsers without WebGL, or with a lost graphics context, receive the same lettering as a static SVG. The optional spacing renderer also stays static.

If a high-density graphics surface produces sustained slow frames, the ribbon gradually reduces its own pixel density towards 1×. Page text, photographs, geometry and movement speed remain unchanged. Geometry is built only after the ribbon font loads, and visible buffer ranges are drawn without rebuilding the mesh when scrolling.

The Home heading starts with posterity and cycles through `headingWords` in `script.js`, including posterity and blog posts. Scrambles last 520ms; the resting interval is 3.8–5 seconds. The word box reserves space so changes do not move the ribbon. Reduced-motion preferences skip scrambling and flower rotation.

`footer.js` and `footer.css` provide the same footer, social links and text copy controls on every page, including `brand-guidelines/`, `payment/`, and `josh-isms/`. The footer contains the logo, social links and copyright. The social controls form a 3×3 square with a centre page number in Space Grotesk. Clicking the number cycles between pages 1 and 2 while retaining keyboard focus and the grid’s position. Page 1 has the eight primary platforms; page 2 has Goodreads and seven subtle grey X marks for empty slots. Empty cells are decorative and skipped by keyboard navigation. Page changes animate all nine marks inward and fade in the replacement set; rapid clicks settle on the requested page. Reduced-motion preferences skip these animations. Hover tilts each platform icon towards the centre of the grid. Active cells retain fading highlights and no hover labels. The enlarged footer logo scales to fit phones. Edit profiles in `footer.js`; additional profiles fill the empty slots automatically. Run `node tools/verify-social-menu.cjs` to check both pages, keyboard interaction, clipboard, overflow, and shared subpage behaviour. Brand guidelines is linked from “personal brand” in the likes list; Payment remains accessible at its existing URL; Contact has been removed. Equal spacing centers the logo between the rules. On narrow screens the links and copyright sit below the logo's lower rule. The sidebar uses `assets/sidebar-logo-new.png`, the supplied `newlogo69.png`, with its transparent padding cropped in CSS.
